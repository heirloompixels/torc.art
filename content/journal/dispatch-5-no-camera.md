<p>I hope that wherever you may be, you were able to watch the full moon rising. </p><p>Tonight may have been one of the most spectacular eye-fulls Ive ever been lucky enoguh to experience. I sat atop a giant granate monolith in northern New Mexico, belaying Kyle up the second pitch of a beautiful meandering route, and watched the moon rise over the Sangre de Christo mountains.   It was one of those moments that engulfs one in wonder and awe at its rapturous, unimaginable beauty.  </p><p>For a moment I had that familiar impulse to capture it in a photo, but alas I had no camera.  It truly could have been one of the best photographs of my lifetime. Everything about that moment was perfection. 0 I kicked myself for not wearing a jacket with zippered pickets into which I could put a phone, or brought a small backpack into which I could have put the camera.  It is hard to break those old habits of wanting to capture it all, and to share the highlights.  But that really isnt the point, and in some ways distracts and detracts from the moment beign experienced. So I consciously shut off that noise in my brain, simply enjoying the beauty and perfection of the moment.</p><p>There needs to be space for both consciously captured moments and quiet fleeting revelry.   That is what we are exploring here. </p><p>This led me into pondering why we create what we create, why does it feel important to share?  My feeling is in the deep inspiration of nature.  I wish to live fully live within the space of wonder and awe for this life we live, to create beautiful and meaningful work (in the myriad mediums that exist at our fingertips) that may inspire the same.  </p>
