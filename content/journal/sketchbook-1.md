<p>Hello everyone from sunny and windy New Mexico.</p><p>Welcome to this first installment of the new Sketchbook series - our intimate behind the scenes email list for our paid supporters of this <em>Intentionally Confusing</em> Project. We will be sticking to a mostly weekly (every friday) dispatch of these emails and they will take various forms, some succinct and some rambling but all wonderful, personal and offering glimpses of our work we don't share with the general public.</p><p><em>Intentionally Confusing</em> is well into it's second year now and we thank you all for your support thus far. This year we feel this experiment is taking shape into what we are finding it to be and we are so excited about that. </p><p>As one of the major perks of being a paid member of <em>Intentionally Confusing</em> is getting preferred status on Jeannie's custom weaving list she is happy to announce she has this morning drawn another name from the small paid supporters list and has sent out a message to this lucky person. Almost all custom orders are now only accessible to those whom are paid supporters and as there are only 31 of you your odds of getting a custom slot are higher than they have ever been! </p><p>This solution to distributing these custom slots feels like the right solution. For years Jeannie has been asked constantly to create custom pieces, so many requests there is no way she could ever weave that many.  It has been very difficult to decide whom to take and whom to deny and over the years she has experimented with so many ideas on how to keep these decisions equitable.  Now, finally, this system feels right!  Supporting this Intentionally Confusing project where we explore new forms of art, expression and creation is appreciated by us more than anything we could ever express.  In return Jeannie is delighted to offer these coveted custom slots to these supporters.  </p><p>This week Jeannie was working on a piece of art yardage which will go up for auction soon and Kyle recorded her working on a particularly tedious passage of tapestry weaving. You can see in this little video snippet below just how long it takes to weave these patterns, how deft her fingers are at manipulating the strings and how much patience and skill it takes to finish.  This act of weaving is just one part of the whole process of creating one of these pieces and it gives a small but illustrative glimpse at why Jeannie could never keep up with the demand for her work.  </p><figure class="kg-card kg-embed-card kg-card-hascaption"><iframe width="200" height="113" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/XW7dSunfIIQ?feature=oembed" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture" allowfullscreen></iframe><figcaption>Jeannie working on a tedious passage of tapestry weaving!</figcaption></figure><p>Our gallery Desert Archaic in Truth or Consequences New Mexico is back in full swing after limping along for the last couple of years during the chaos of COVID.  We are humbled by the support of both our local and international community for helping us to keep this magical space open during these strange times.  This space is very special to us as it's the physical embodiment of the glimpses we share online.  We truly change it up monthly and there are always strange experiments on view there.  </p><p>The ability to share art with this rural New Mexico community is very important to us as we resoundingly believe that art is one of the most important parts of life. We constantly show works, experiments and performances in this space in order to bring new ideas and ways of seeing the world to the people whom live in this oasis on the banks of the Rio Grande. </p><p>One show on view now is Mycelium Mysterium - a combined show of Jeannie's mushroom collage women and Kyle's intaglio printmaking. The combination of these two bodies of work is a great illustration of what we are attempting to harmonize with this <em>Intentionally Confusing</em> project. Though we both create wonderful work on our own the interation of our work creates something fuller than the individual parts.     </p><figure class="kg-card kg-embed-card kg-card-hascaption"><iframe width="200" height="113" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/KQaENKS5qu0?feature=oembed" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture" allowfullscreen></iframe><figcaption>Kyle installing Mycelium Mysterium at Desert Archaic Gallery.</figcaption></figure><p>One final digression before we go: these emails are written by both of us in a collaborative back and forth writing process. You will notice that we slip in and out of first and third person depending upon the context of what is being written. Determining which "voice" to use in these emails is an unexpected challenge in the whole process which we have not fully resolved.   </p><p>Thank you for supporting and following along with this project. </p><p>Jeannie &amp; Kyle </p>
