Thirst became known.</p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card"><img src="https://media.torc.art/dispatches/no-5/P4284090.webp" class="kg-image" loading="lazy" width="1600" height="1200"></figure><p>A considerable amount of our leisure has been given to exploring the greater Gila over the last decade and yet there is always so much more mystery just down another canyon.  Time stands still here and existence is given over to the birds, trout, snakes, lizards, coyote, elk, bear, wolf, deer, javelina and all mighty squirrel. (these, with extra fluffy ears) Beaver, racoon and eagles.  Trees. Ancient Trees. Wise Trees. Life stubbornly abides everywhere else. Eyes closed on the rim rocks of a fork of the Gila River, the river sounds below rush up, greeting the wind-sky sounds of the mesa top resulting in the resonant sound of time. </p><p>Our experiences in these mountains, and mesas, and canyons inform all that we make; there is a part of the Gila in everything we make. </p><figure class="kg-card kg-gallery-card kg-width-wide"><div class="kg-gallery-container"><div class="kg-gallery-row"><div class="kg-gallery-image"><img src="https://media.torc.art/dispatches/no-5/P5024158.webp" width="1600" height="2134" loading="lazy"></div><div class="kg-gallery-image"><img src="https://media.torc.art/dispatches/no-5/P4294099.webp" width="1600" height="1200" loading="lazy"></div><div class="kg-gallery-image"><img src="https://media.torc.art/dispatches/no-5/P4284082.webp" width="1600" height="1200" loading="lazy"></div></div></div></figure><p>We visited the Gila a few days ago and found it dry this year like we have never known it to be.  The Southwest is dry.  The west is dry.  Fires loom on the horizon, smoke lingers if you squint into the sunset.  We are worried for this place left to observe helplessly the cycle of time we now live in.  </p>
