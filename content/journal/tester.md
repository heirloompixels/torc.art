<p>This is our journey along the Grand Enchantment Train, lso known as the first thing that brought us to Truth or Consequences, New Mexico. The winter and spring of 2010 was a wet one in the Southwest, high mountains were blanketed in feet of white and the low desert wore a cloak of flowers like no one had seen in decades. The <a href="https://www.simblissity.net/get-home.shtml">GET</a> traverses 770 miles of high mountains and low desert in Arizona and New Mexico from Phoenix to Albuquerque. </p><p>We spent the winter leading up to that spring in fridgid northern climates, living in my (Jeannie) grandparents basemement as I cared for my Grandma in her last months of life. It was an intensely beautiful-difficult time, and the only thing we wanted to do at the end of it was walk.  Walk to process.  Walk to explore. Walk to warm our bones in the desert sun. </p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card kg-card-hascaption"><img src="https://media.torc.art/dispatches/tester/P3313182.webp" class="kg-image" alt="Yellow poppy flowers carpet the desert floor with fuzzy-spikes cactus in the background" loading="lazy" width="1600" height="1200"><figcaption>Cholla, Saguaros and Desert Gold Poppies in the Sonoran Desert.&nbsp;</figcaption></figure><p>On April 4th my brother dropped us of at the edge of the Superstition Mountains east of Phoenix and we started walking.  In keeping with our true style we just jumped into it, only having only a stack of topo maps, a vague idea of what we were getting into, and carrying way to much stuff (food and art supplies namely).  The way is remote, and aside from the few small towns with grocery stores, you need to mail yourself boxes of food so as not to get stranded in Klondyke, AZ with nothing but a bag of nuts to your name. So we mailed boxes of food to a few choice locations, and walked. </p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card kg-card-hascaption"><img src="https://media.torc.art/dispatches/tester/P3303162.webp" class="kg-image" alt="A hand holding a small ball of cactus thorns to the sky" loading="lazy" width="1600" height="1200"><figcaption>Dried cactus remnants</figcaption></figure><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card kg-card-hascaption"><img src="https://media.torc.art/dispatches/tester/P4043356.webp" class="kg-image" alt="A desert scene with saguaro cactus, mesquite tree and two yucca that have flowered and died" loading="lazy" width="1600" height="1200"><figcaption>Perfectly poised plants along the way, a Saguaro and two Yucca align in a very slow dance.&nbsp;</figcaption></figure><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card kg-card-hascaption"><img src="https://media.torc.art/dispatches/tester/P4053365.webp" class="kg-image" alt="A man and woman standing hand in hand in a desert mountain landscape. " loading="lazy" width="1600" height="1200"><figcaption>Day two, saguarro rib walking stick in hand, the desert rolling out before us.&nbsp;</figcaption></figure><p>This was my first year with a digital camera.  Before this I was a strict lover of analog phototography, black &amp; white silver gelatin, small format, medium format and large format....any of them were good with me! But now we had technology, and a timer, so the novelty of getting photos of both of us was quite exciting. </p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card kg-card-hascaption"><img src="https://media.torc.art/dispatches/tester/P4053368.webp" class="kg-image" alt="Two backpacks sit on the ground at a fork in the trail. " loading="lazy" width="1600" height="1200"><figcaption>Time to check the topos, which fork do we choose? Little did we know this would be the easiest route finding on the entire mission.&nbsp;</figcaption></figure><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card kg-card-hascaption"><img src="https://media.torc.art/dispatches/tester/P4053380.webp" class="kg-image" alt="profile of a woman at night wearing a headlamp, holding a yellow marshmallow peep on a stick" loading="lazy" width="1600" height="1200"><figcaption>When someone gives you peeps, you roast them over a fire.&nbsp;</figcaption></figure><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card"><img src="https://media.torc.art/dispatches/tester/P4063383-1.webp" class="kg-image" loading="lazy" width="1600" height="2134"></figure><p>I hope that you never have to bushwhack down a mountain through a thicket of <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegalia_greggii">Catsclaw</a>.  Its like a million baby pumas digging into you from all sides, and having nowhere to go but forward, into more razor sharp claws.  After descending down a barely-there trail, compleatly overgrown with Catsclaw, we landed in a riparian paradise.  Water, giant sycamores and ancient dwellings.  We nearly stayed, said forget the walking, lets just stay here in this place of perfection until we run out of food and then move onto the next thing.  ....needless to say that didnt happen, but the idea was seriously pondered. We spent the night, and walked on. </p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card kg-card-hascaption"><img src="https://media.torc.art/dispatches/tester/P4063407.webp" class="kg-image" alt="A landscape in the desert, the ground covered with thick green vegetation, the mesquite trees lining the trail just twigs without leaves. " loading="lazy" width="1600" height="1200"><figcaption>In impossible carpet of green in the desert, what a treat! It was so early in the season the Mesquites had not yet leafed out. From here we descended into the town of Superior, AZ and quickly headed back into the hills.&nbsp;</figcaption></figure><p>We stayed in the little town of Superior only long enough to stock up on food and mail a package of things we didn't need to a relative's house. Within the first days of walking we realized there was going to be no time for sitting in the shade of a mesquite and making art.  There was too much walking to be done in between water sources, and when it was time to eat and rest, we were too tired.  Looking back on it now I cant beleive how much weight we carried, despite ditching the art supplies.  </p><p>Now is not the moment to dive in the world of lightweight backpacking nerdiness, (There will be a whole other page about that coming before too long - LINK HERE) but I will say that since the Grand Enchantment Trail we have traded in our steel pots for titanium ones, a 90s tent for a more modern hyperlite one and wool blankets for puffy-pants. Though our love of hiking in sandals hasn't changed, we now hike in Lunas instead of Chacos.  </p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card kg-card-hascaption"><img src="https://media.torc.art/dispatches/tester/P4073420.webp" class="kg-image" loading="lazy" width="1600" height="1200"><figcaption>Remnants of ancient beings greet us as we leave Superior and head back out into the Sonoran Desert.&nbsp;</figcaption></figure><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card kg-card-hascaption"><img src="https://media.torc.art/dispatches/tester/P4073426.webp" class="kg-image" alt="Man sitting by a trail looking at a topographical map with giant cliffs and cactus in the background " loading="lazy" width="1600" height="1200"><figcaption>There were many, many moments like this. Catching a little shade to examine the topo maps.</figcaption></figure><p>Before departure we printed all of the topos for the route on regular printer paper. So as not to carry all of them from the begining we shipped the relavent sections with the boxes of food that we shipped to the remote spots along the way.  As we passed through each mapped section we used our crumpled and dirty guides as firestarter.  Oh yes, and on this journey we cooked only over fire, we brought no stove. </p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card kg-card-hascaption"><img src="https://media.torc.art/dispatches/tester/P4073428.webp" class="kg-image" alt="Man wearing a large backpack standing on a trail, looking at an enormous saguaro cactus. " loading="lazy" width="1600" height="2134"><figcaption>Magnificant Giants. The only thing that stops us from putting on the miles, is everything. Everything must be inspected and admired.&nbsp;</figcaption></figure><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card kg-card-hascaption"><img src="https://media.torc.art/dispatches/tester/P4083442.webp" class="kg-image" loading="lazy" width="1600" height="1200"><figcaption>The Shakuachi was one thing that Kyle couldn't part with when lightening our loads. Moments of rest were lifted with the flowing sounds of this instrument.&nbsp;</figcaption></figure><p></p>
