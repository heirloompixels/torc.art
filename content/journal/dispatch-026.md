<p>The Cold Moon is rising through a thinly clouded sky here in southern New Mexico. The air is crisp and the bird baths will surely freeze tonight, though likely they will thaw again by tomorrow afternoon. The daylight is precious here in our studio-life. </p><p>Today begins the 12th year we have spent at our tiny little home in Truth or Consequences, New Mexico. I remember that first night staying up late drinking a $10 bottle of bubbles which we bought simply because it had gnomes on it and sleeping on the floor to wake up to the morning sunlight and cacophony of bird song. We felt rooted and excited to create a new world out of the thin pure air of the desert.</p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card kg-card-hascaption"><img src="https://media.torc.art/dispatches/dispatch-026/AF756A23-20E2-4B81-83DF-75A03FAF7685.webp" class="kg-image" alt="A woman sits at a loom weaving in the corner of a room next to a bright window. " loading="lazy" width="960" height="720"><figcaption>Jeannie weaving in the front room of our tiny house in December of 2011.</figcaption></figure><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card kg-card-hascaption"><img src="https://media.torc.art/dispatches/dispatch-026/BE0A3645-82E2-411F-AEE3-4B82A8520F58_1_201_a.webp" class="kg-image" alt="A yellow wall fills most of the picture while a man sitting on the floor looks down at his hands. " loading="lazy" width="1600" height="1200"><figcaption>Kyle drawing in the front room of our house in December 2011.</figcaption></figure><p>Eleven years later we wake up this morning to a monstrous skeleton of a house sheathed in plywood the day before blotting out last nights full moon rise and this mornings sun rise. Our hearts have been heavy for the last week as we've watched a crew frame up this building and realized just how dramatically our space, created with our own two hands, was in flux. </p><p>Yesterday a solid form dramatically and permanently changed the physical space we have existed in the for last 11 years. Today's anniversary feels much more like the start of a new chapter in our life then it does another year in Truth or Consequences. A spell has been broken as we look around and see just what is the next phase of life for us. Curious synchronicity of these events on this same day years apart. </p><p>It is hard to not feel the sinking of my heart as I hear the air hammers blast over the clacking of my loom and the audio book in my earbuds as they build a house to the east of me, 10 feet to my left, 30 feet above, blocking both my view of the mountains and sky with two by fours and plywood. I did not move to the desert to be boxed in, to look out my window and see nothing but man made forms. In a strange way i feel defeated as if they have taken something sacred away from me. </p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card kg-card-hascaption"><img src="https://media.torc.art/dispatches/dispatch-026/5BFC0662-8ECE-46C6-90DC-8CEF16E5CEC8_1_102_o.webp" class="kg-image" alt="A overgrown backyard with a loom and canvas on easel setup as a workspace for artists." loading="lazy" width="1600" height="1200"><figcaption>Our Studio circa 2013: weaving and painting in our back yard.</figcaption></figure><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card kg-card-hascaption"><img src="https://media.torc.art/dispatches/dispatch-026/4CF04089-A71C-4A43-9C49-105C949D84D5_1_102_o.webp" class="kg-image" alt="A woman sits at a loom weaving in an unfinished building with various objects on shelves behind her." loading="lazy" width="1600" height="1200"><figcaption>Jeannie in the shell of our studio circa 2014</figcaption></figure><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card kg-card-hascaption"><img src="https://media.torc.art/dispatches/dispatch-026/5249A3D0-54C4-4733-ABF6-A3200379F842_1_102_o.webp" class="kg-image" alt="A man sits on the ground painting in the doorway of an unfinished building." loading="lazy" width="1600" height="1200"><figcaption>Kyle painting in the future doorway of our studio circa 2014.&nbsp;</figcaption></figure><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card kg-card-hascaption"><img src="https://media.torc.art/dispatches/dispatch-026/C6332167-C0FA-4ABC-B4D3-79585D5A41B0_1_105_c.webp" class="kg-image" alt="A man digs with a shovel in a dirt covered yard with a tree to one side and a shed in the background." loading="lazy" width="1024" height="768"><figcaption>Kyle digging the first garden bed in 2012.&nbsp;</figcaption></figure><p></p><p>Life is constant like that with the change. It seems stasis can hold in the illusion of timelessness. Eleven years of creating, building and growing seems like the blink of an eye. Yet the days were long, each and every one of them - full to the brim with life. A long daydream of adventure and creation and chores and fun. Endlessly unfolding, slowly permuting and ever comfortable. For a bit it's like you've been wrapped up and tucked into a warm bean and cheese burrito and everything is just cozy. Then the world dips you in the ultra hot sauce and it stings your eyes.</p><p>I think the biggest takeaway from all this is that time is insidiously slippery and we should all take care to fully engage with every single moment.</p><hr><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card kg-card-hascaption"><img src="https://media.torc.art/dispatches/dispatch-026/C6FBB707-01DF-4E31-9880-B7C9D812652D_1_102_a.webp" class="kg-image" alt="A white wall with jewelry hung off wooden forms spaced intentionally and filling the wall." loading="lazy" width="1600" height="1220"><figcaption>The Movement of Stone</figcaption></figure><p>In 2019 Jeannie dreamed up a project which has only just now come to fruition. It is an interaction with geologic time on the scale of human hands. </p><p>A heart beats, feet step, rain falls, stones roll.</p><p><a href="https://jeannieortiz.com/the-movement-of-stone/">The Movement of Stone</a> is a project begun in 2019 composed of 32 pieces of adornment. The pieces constructed in this series trace a path traveled in that pre-pandemic year. They are small meditations on our human bodies, the spaces we traverse, and the elements of earth that pass through our hands. Of the places I set foot, each stone was carefully selected, every piece brought home was drilled and used in this project.</p><p>On this day, as the moon flew in its orbit below the horizon she installed this series in <a href="https://www.instagram.com/desertarchaic/?hl=en">Desert Archaic Gallery</a>, here in Truth or Consequences, New Mexico. Each piece of adornment hangs from the gallery wall from a wooden form that was also hand made by Jeannie. The frames and minimalist hangers are made from wood of walnut, oak, maple and pine.</p><p>The pieces are all available for sale in person in Desert Archaic, and will be available online in a week or so.
