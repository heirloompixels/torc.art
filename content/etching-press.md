# Community Etching Press

Our community etching press makes the equipment of printmaking and bookbinding available to artists of the southwest and beyond.

We are pleased to offer time on our etching press to artists in our community. Intaglio printmaking is one of the oldest forms of printmaking — the technique originated in the fifteenth century. This press enables artists to create stunning hand crafted prints, each of which is a unique work of art.

In 2025, when [Smithsonian Magazine named Truth or Consequences one of the 15 best small towns in the USA](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/travel/the-15-best-small-towns-to-visit-in-2025-180986681/), the community etching press was one of the reasons they gave.

## Why a community press

The press is the oldest ambition this gallery has. In 2017, when the collective that became this gallery was still called SEBA, a small DIY grant from Meow Wolf paid for a letterpress found at a local auction, and by the next winter we were teaching printing workshops that we described at the time as "open to the public and trials for much bigger ambitions" — the long-run goal, written into the grant application, was "the foundation of a printmaking studio." This is that studio.

When we bought 312 Main in 2023 we wrote down what we wanted the building to be: a space with "ample space and access to all of the requisite tools," shared with local artists, drawing in artists from afar. A press is the clearest form of that idea. Almost nobody can own one — they weigh tons and cost a fortune — but everybody can use one. One good press, held in common, turns a whole town of people who draw into a town of printmakers.

## What drypoint is

An image is drawn on a plate by physically scratching it with a scribe point. The plate is inked, then wiped clean, leaving ink deposited in the scratches. The plate, with a piece of wet paper laid upon it, is rolled through the press, where an extraordinary amount of pressure pushes the wet paper into the incised lines and transfers the ink held there. The prints are then dried and pressed flat. Color, when there is color, is added afterward by hand and brush.

The scratched lines are fragile and collapse slowly under the pressure of the press, so a drypoint plate yields only a few dozen prints before it is exhausted. The edition size is not a marketing decision — it is a fact about the medium.

## Press Usage

All artists must participate in a 2 hour orientation program. The first hour is used to familiarize you with our space, where things are and what is expected of you to use the space. The second hour of the program is a hands on printing session guided by one of our staff members, at the end of which each artist will be allowed to work on the press by themselves.

We have weekly open printing sessions, usually on Saturdays 11–1 and 2–4. Up to six artists can work during each two hour printing block. Artists booking both the early and late printing session can print all day.

On weekdays private press time can be booked based on availability. Private classes are available.

Open sessions pause while we are out doing our summer Fieldwork — if you are planning a trip around the press, email ahead and we will make sure it happens.

## Ink

Only water soluble inks are used in the studio. We provide black ink with use of the press. Other colors are often available — please talk to us about current options, or to recommend more colors to stock.

Artists are invited to bring their own inks but it must be Akua Intaglio Ink unless pre-approved by us. The Akua inks are wonderful, easy to work with, and very easy to clean. We recommend them for most users.

We are willing to work with advanced users who would like to use specific inks but we need to talk about this first in order to keep the studio clean and safe.

## Printer's Proofs

In order to offset the costs of using the press we will trade time on the press for prints from artists, which we then sell through our Printer's Proofs editions. This is a needs based program — please talk to us and we will work out a way to get you printing that works for both of us.

The trade is older than the program. The collective this gallery grew from survived on, in its own words, "hard work, self resilience, and a supporting community of friends that help each other and barter as a way to survive." Printer's Proofs is that same economy, formalized: if money is the thing between you and the press, bring prints instead.

## The bindery

Printmaking is half of what lives here. The other half is a working letterpress and bindery: a Vandercook 325G proof press — moved in by forklift, then rolled the last stretch Egyptian-style on one-inch pipes, and now permanently installed — a small tabletop jobbing press, a Kensol foil stamper for spine labels, a cast-iron board shear, a guillotine cutter, nipping presses, and cases of metal type. This is the equipment behind [Agile Meteor Press](/agile-meteor-press/), whose special editions are bound in house with the help of community volunteers.

Letterpress and bookbinding workshops run on this equipment — the first ones in 2018, more planned. If you want to learn to set type or sew a book, [get in touch](/visit/).
